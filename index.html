Why a mini brain?

A human brain has 86 billion neurons and 100 trillion connections. That's about 10,000× more than a laptop can hold.

So we build small circuits instead. Each one is just big enough to do one real brain job.

Three ideas to start with

1A neuron is a leaky bucket.It fills with input and fires a spike when full.
2A synapse is a volume knob.Learning means turning knobs up or down.
3Regions are specialist teams.Vision, memory and movement, wired together.

Where this leads

The same ideas you build here power some of today's most exciting technology, and some of its most hopeful uses.

Neuromorphic computing

Chips built from spiking neurons and synapses, like Intel's Loihi 2 and SpiNNaker. They aim for the brain's efficiency: about 20 W for everything you do.

Builds on Steps 1–3

Brain–computer interfaces

Neuralink, Synchron, BrainGate and others read spikes to let people with paralysis control computers. Since 2023, research implants have decoded attempted speech into text.

Builds on Steps 1–2

Brain-inspired AI

Hopfield networks earned the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics. Today's AI still borrows ideas from memory, attention and learning in the brain.

Builds on Steps 2 and 4

Medicine

Digital twins of a patient's brain to plan epilepsy surgery, computer-tested drug safety, and implants that stop seizures as they start.

Builds on Steps 1 and 3

Organoid intelligence

Living neurons grown in a dish learned to play the video game Pong in 2022. Researchers now study how such cultures learn, and whether they could ever compute usefully.

Builds on Step 2

Neurorights

Brain data is deeply personal. Chile (2021) and Colorado (2024) passed laws to protect it. As these tools spread, privacy and consent matter as much as the science.
