How connected is the brain?

The brain's power lives less in its neurons than in their connections. Here's how many there are, how they're arranged, and what it would take to copy them.

The most common neuron in your brain (the cerebellar granule cell, ~69 billion of them) has just 4 inputs, while its neighbour the Purkinje cell has ~150,000. So the brain-wide average, ~1,700, hides a 40,000× range.

Why the brain is wired this way

    
    
      
  • Local teams (high clustering) can work on one job together, like a cortical column.
    • 
      
  • A few shortcuts (low hops) let distant regions share results fast.
    • 
      
  • Wire is expensive. Long axons cost space and energy, so the brain uses as few as it can. Small-world wiring gets both benefits cheaply.

More than connections

  
Even with the right number of links, a real brain has layers of complexity that most models leave out.

  

    
Dendrites compute
One cortical neuron's branches behave like a small multi-layer network (a 2021 study needed 5–8 layers to mimic one).
Mini Brain: single points

    
Thousands of cell types
A 2023 atlas of the mouse brain found over 5,000 genetically distinct cell types.
Mini Brain: 2 (excitatory, inhibitory)

    
Chemical moods
Dopamine, serotonin and others reshape whole circuits within seconds.
Mini Brain: coming in Step 7

    
Unreliable synapses
Many synapses fail to release on 50–90% of spikes, and that may help the brain learn.
Mini Brain: always reliable

    
Travel delays
Spikes take from under 1 ms to tens of ms to arrive, and timing shapes rhythms.
Mini Brain: instant

    
Glia
About as many glial cells as neurons. They feed, insulate, and even tune synapses.
Mini Brain: none

    
Learning at every timescale
From milliseconds (short-term changes) to years (structural growth).
Mini Brain: STDP in Step 2

    
Growth and pruning
Synapses peak in early childhood, then up to half are pruned in some regions by adulthood.
Mini Brain: fixed wiring

  


  
Do spiking networks have this?

  

    
AI-style spiking networks
ConnectionsHundreds to thousands per neuron, in rigid layers.
MissingLocal recurrent wiring, cell types, dendrites, chemistry.

    
Neuromorphic chips
ConnectionsLimited by hardware: TrueNorth 256 per neuron; Hala Point ~110 on average.
MissingBrain-like fan-in. They trade links for scale and energy savings.

    
Brain-scale simulations
ConnectionsRealistic: thousands per neuron (e.g. the 2013 K-computer run, ~6,000).
MissingSpeed (40 minutes per second of brain), and still simple point neurons.

  


  
Beyond the lab

  

    
Emerging tech: mapping every connection

    
    
      
  • FlyWire (2024) mapped a whole fruit fly brain: about 139,000 neurons and 50 million synapses.
    • 
      
  • H01 (2024) mapped 1 mm³ of human cortex: about 57,000 cells and 150 million synapses, in 1.4 petabytes of images.
    • 
      
  • MICrONS (2025) mapped 1 mm³ of mouse visual cortex, with over 500 million synapses, and recorded what those neurons did.
    • 
    

  

  

    
For good

    
    
      
  • Safer brain surgery. MRI tractography maps major wiring bundles so surgeons can avoid them when removing tumours.
    • 
      
  • Understanding disease. Comparing wiring diagrams helps researchers study conditions where connectivity may differ, from epilepsy to neurodevelopmental disorders.
    • 
      
  • Efficient AI. Learning from the brain's cheap wiring is one route to computers that use far less energy.
    • 
    

  


  
Key terms

  

    

      
Fan-in / fan-out
Number of inputs a neuron receives / outputs it sends.

      
Connectome
A complete wiring diagram of a nervous system.

      
Small-world network
Tight local clusters plus a few shortcuts: few hops, strong teams.

      
Clustering
How often a neuron's partners are also linked to each other.

      
Path length
Average number of hops between two neurons.

      
Synaptic event
One spike arriving at one synapse: the unit of simulation work.
