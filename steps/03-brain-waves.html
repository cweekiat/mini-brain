The idea

A brain wave is like a crowd falling into clapping in sync. Nobody leads. Each person just reacts to the people near them.

  • 80% of neurons are accelerators (excitatory). They make others fire.
  • 20% are brakes (inhibitory). They silence others.
  • Push, brake, release, repeat: the loop between them is the rhythm.

Where brain waves come from

  

    
1. What an EEG picks up

    
An EEG electrode on the scalp can't hear single spikes. It picks up the synaptic currents of millions of neurons at once.

    
    
      
  • In sync: currents add up into a wave you can measure.
    • 
      
  • Out of sync: they cancel out, and the EEG looks flat.
    • 
    

    
So a brain wave is a sign of timing, not of how busy the brain is. That's why Calm looked flat even though neurons kept firing.

  

  

    
2. The gamma loop, in four beats

    
    
      
  1. Accelerators fire together.Excitatory (pyramidal) cells send a volley.
    2. 
      
  2. Brakes fire a moment later.Fast-spiking interneurons are kicked by that volley.
    3. 
      
  3. Everyone is silenced by GABA.For about as long as inhibition lasts (τI).
    4. 
      
  4. The brake wears off and the loop restarts.One cycle ≈ 30 ms, so about 33 cycles per second: gamma.
    5. 
    

  

  

    
3. The bands

    

      
Delta0.5–4 HzDeep sleep

      
Theta4–8 HzMemory, navigation

      
Alpha8–13 HzRelaxed, eyes closed

      
Beta13–30 HzActive thinking, movement

      
Gamma30–100 HzFocused attention

    

    
Each band comes from different loops and cell types. Our network makes the beta–gamma kind.

The equations

  

    
Equation 1 · Every neuron

    
The leaky bucket from Step 1, now fed by the network.

    

      τm · dVj/dt = −(Vj − EL)
      + R (Iext + ηj + IE,j − II,j)
    

    

      
IextDrive from the rest of the brain (different for E and I cells).

      
ηjNoise: random jostling, a fresh value every step.

      
IE,jExcitatory synaptic current into neuron j.

      
II,jInhibitory synaptic current. It is subtracted.

    

  

  

    
Equation 2 · Every synapse

    
Step 2's synapse, summed over all inputs.

    

      τE dIE/dt = −IE,  +wE per E spike
      τI dII/dt = −II,  +g·wE per I spike
    

    

      
τE = 3 msHow long excitation (AMPA) lasts.

      
τI = 8 msHow long inhibition (GABAA) lasts. Sets the rhythm's speed.

      
gHow much stronger a brake is than an accelerator.

      
10%Chance that any two neurons are connected (about 100 inputs each).

    

  

  

    
Equation 3 · The EEG-like signal

    

      EEG(t) = mean over E cells of (IE + II)
    

    
Real EEG mostly reflects synaptic currents in excitatory (pyramidal) cells, so we add up their input currents, ignoring sign. This is a common simple stand-in.

Beyond the lab

  

    
Emerging tech

    
    
      
  • Implants that listen and respond. The NeuroPace RNS device (FDA-approved 2013) watches brain waves for the start of a seizure and delivers a small pulse to stop it.
    • 
      
  • 40 Hz light and sound. Flickering at gamma frequency is being tested in clinical trials as a possible Alzheimer's treatment, after promising results in mice.
    • 
      
  • Low-power wearables. Neuromorphic chips can watch EEG for seizure patterns while using very little battery.
    • 
    

  

  

    
For good

    
    
      
  • Epilepsy surgery planning. “Digital twin” models of a patient's own brain network (for example, the Virtual Epileptic Patient) help surgeons predict where seizures start.
    • 
      
  • Sleep and anaesthesia. Hospitals read brain waves to track sleep stages and depth of anaesthesia.
    • 
    

  


  
Key terms

  

    

      
Brain wave (oscillation)
Rhythmic rise and fall in many neurons' activity.

      
EEG
Electrodes on the scalp recording summed brain currents.

      
Excitatory / inhibitory neuron
Makes its targets more / less likely to fire.

      
Interneuron
A local, usually inhibitory neuron. Fast-spiking ones drive gamma.

      
E/I balance
Excitation held in check by inhibition. Losing it can cause seizures.

      
Synchrony
Neurons firing at the same moments.

      
Raster plot
One row per neuron, one dot per spike.

      
Power spectrum
How much of each frequency a signal contains.
