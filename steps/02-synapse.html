The idea

A synapse is a volume knob between two neurons. When neuron A fires, B gets a small push. The knob sets how big the push is.

  • One push is rarely enough. Several must add up to fire B.
  • Pushes can be negative. Inhibitory synapses quiet B down.
  • The knob turns itself. If A keeps firing just before B, it gets louder.

How a real synapse works

  

    
1. Passing the message

    
    
      
  1. Spike arrives at A's axon tip.Calcium rushes in.
    2. 
      
  2. Transmitter is released (glutamate) in under 1 ms.It drifts across a 20 nm gap.
    3. 
      
  3. Receptors on B open and let sodium in.B gets a small bump: an EPSP, about 0.5–1 mV.
    4. 
      
  4. The bump fades in 10–20 ms.Transmitter is cleared away, ready for the next spike.
    5. 
    

    
Inhibitory synapses release GABA instead. It lets chloride in and pushes B down (an IPSP).

  

  

    
2. Learning: the NMDA receptor

    
One receptor, NMDA, is a coincidence detector. It only opens when both happen:

    
    
      
  • A just released glutamate, and
    • 
      
  • B is already firing (which pops out a magnesium plug).
    • 
    

    
Then calcium floods into B, and B adds more receptors: the synapse gets stronger (LTP). If B fired first, a small calcium trickle removes receptors instead: weaker (LTD).

    
This is Hebb's 1949 idea, “fire together, wire together”, with a twist: order matters.

The equations

  

    
Equation 1 · The synapse

    
Each spike from A adds a kick that fades away.

    

      τs · dIsyn/dt = −Isyn
      at each A spike: Isyn ← Isyn + w
    

    

      
IsynCurrent flowing into B through the synapse.

      
wThe weight: the volume knob. Negative = inhibitory.

      
τs = 5 msHow fast the receptors close again.

    

    
B is the leaky bucket from Step 1, with Isyn added to its input.

  

  

    
Equation 2 · The learning rule (STDP)

    
The weight change depends on the gap Δt = tB − tA.

    

      Δt > 0:  Δw = +A+ e−Δt/τ+
      Δt < 0:  Δw = −A− e+Δt/τ−
    

    

      
Δt > 0A fired first and may have helped cause B. Strengthen.

      
Δt < 0B fired first, so A couldn't have caused it. Weaken.

      
A+, A−Maximum change per pair (0.80 and 0.85).

      
τ = 20 msHow fast the effect fades with a bigger gap.

    

    
In code, each neuron keeps a fading “trace” of its last spike. Reading the other neuron's trace gives e−|Δt|/τ without storing any spike times.

Key terms

  

    

      
Synapse
The junction where one neuron passes a signal to another.

      
Presynaptic / postsynaptic
The sending neuron (A) / the receiving neuron (B).

      
Neurotransmitter
The chemical messenger: glutamate excites, GABA inhibits.

      
EPSP / IPSP
A small push up / down in B's voltage after one input spike.

      
Synaptic weight
How strong a connection is.

      
Summation
Bumps adding up when they arrive close together.

      
LTP / LTD
Long-term potentiation / depression: lasting strengthening / weakening.

      
STDP
Learning rule where spike order and timing decide LTP or LTD.
